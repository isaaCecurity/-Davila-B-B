The design language behind BakeFlow.

BakeFlow is the bakery's command center, so its interface has one job: make a business legible at a glance. Everything here follows from that — money is the loudest thing on screen, structure is carried by surface and space rather than lines and boxes, and motion only ever explains where something came from. Warm, calm, confident. Never clinical, never cute.

01 — Foundation

Colour

A warm, low-noise palette. Deep Cocoa carries all primary weight; Warm Cream is the ground the whole product sits on. Baked Apricot is the single accent and is rationed deliberately — when everything is highlighted, nothing is. Semantic colours appear only to state a fact about the business, never for decoration.

02 — Foundation

Typography

Inter throughout, tracked tight at display sizes. The scale is deliberately short — eight steps, no in-between sizes — so hierarchy comes from decisive jumps rather than fifteen shades of nearly-the-same. Nothing that carries meaning is ever smaller than 11.5px.

Numbers are the interface

Financial figures are set in tabular numerals so digits never shift as values change or animate. A figure always states what it is directly beneath it, at a fraction of the size — the number leads, the label follows.

03 — Foundation

Spacing

A strict 8pt system. Every gap, pad and inset is one of these values, which is what makes screens feel settled without anyone being able to say why. Screen gutter is 20px; related things sit 12px apart, separate ideas 24px or more.

04 — Foundation

Radius

Corner softness scales with the size of the surface: small controls stay tight, cards relax, sheets are generously round. Consistency here is what makes the product feel tactile rather than drawn.

05 — Foundation

Elevation

Shadows are whisper-quiet — they exist to lift a surface off the cream, not to announce themselves. Depth is used sparingly: a card is barely raised, a bottom sheet is clearly floating, and nothing in between needs to compete.

06 — Foundation

Motion

Motion is how the product explains itself. Every transition answers "where did this come from?" — pushes come from the right, sheets rise from the bottom, numbers count to their new value so a change is felt rather than just displayed. Nothing bounces, nothing spins for decoration, and everything respects prefers-reduced-motion.

07 — Identity

Brand & the flow motif

The BakeFlow mark — a hand-drawn loaf with rising steam — is the warm, human anchor of the identity. Alongside it the product carries one abstract signature: a soft flow curve, standing for dough, movement and the flow of the business. It appears as the curve of every revenue chart, as the composition bar that shows where money went, and as the quiet gesture in empty states. It is never decoration for its own sake.

08 — Components

Buttons

One primary action per screen, stated in Deep Cocoa. Apricot is reserved for the moment an action is genuinely the point of the screen. Every button responds to touch with a subtle press — the interface should feel like it has physical substance.

09 — Components

Inputs & controls

Fields are surfaces, not outlined boxes — a filled input on cream reads as something you can type into without needing a border to prove it. Money entry gets its own oversized treatment, because in this product entering an amount is the most common thing anyone does.

10 — Components

Cards & data display

Cards group meaning; they are not containers for their own sake. The dark hero panel is used exactly once per screen, for the single number that screen exists to communicate. Everything else recedes.

11 — Components

Status & feedback

Every status badge pairs a glyph with a word — colour alone never carries meaning, so the interface still works for colour-blind users and in harsh Lagos sunlight. Sync and error states stay calm and plain-spoken: they say what happened and what to do, never what failed internally.

12 — Components

Charts

Charts are drawn, not plotted. No gridlines, no axis furniture, no legends explaining what colour means — just the shape of the business, with the value revealed on touch. The revenue curve uses the BakeFlow flow curve; comparison periods appear as a quiet dashed ghost behind it.

13 — Components

Icons

One family, drawn on a 24px grid at 1.65px stroke with round caps and joins. Geometric and calm, never illustrative. Icons label actions and objects; they never replace a word that matters.

14 — Patterns

Navigation & role-adaptive IA

BakeFlow does not ship one dashboard with buttons hidden per role — the information hierarchy itself changes. An owner opens into profit; a manager into today's operations and the cash drawer; counter staff into the next task; a driver into their run. The tab bar is rebuilt per role, so nobody navigates around features that were never theirs.

15 — Architecture

Component inventory

The prototype is built from a fixed set of reusable pieces, each mapping cleanly onto a React Native component so this translates to Expo + NativeWind without redesign.
